Background & Context

Food adulteration remains common and risky for public health. The project builds a data-driven way to surface high-risk products, adulterants, and brands so regulators can prioritize inspections and actions.

Approach, Method & Strategy

Dataset of 1,000 recorded adulteration cases was processed using Python and Google Sheets for cleaning and Tableau for visualization. Analysis focused on frequency, severity, and product–adulterant correlation. Insights were summarized into an interactive dashboard for regulatory decision-making.

  • Tools: Python (pandas), Google Sheets, Tableau
  • Process: Data Cleaning → Exploratory Data Analysis → Visualization → Insights & Recommendations
  • Scope: 1000 food adulteration reports on Jan 2024 — Jun 2024

Results & Key Metrics

  • Juice (124), Chicken (120), and Milk (107) — top high-risk products
  • Most common adulterants: Coloring agents (215), Chalk (202), Artificial sweeteners (196), Melamine (194), Water (193)
  • Brand C recorded 222 cases — the most frequent offender

Recommendations

  • Prioritize monitoring of coloring agents & chalk (highest severity & frequency)
  • Conduct regular and more frequent inspections, especially in non-festive periods
  • Tighten enforcement: fines, product recalls, and penalties for repeat offenders
  • Improve consumer awareness
